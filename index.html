Privacy & safety

Designed for protected
clinical workflows.

FLOadmit’s clinical deployment plan includes controlled access, protected processing, traceable reports, and a managed data lifecycle. These controls must be configured and verified with the hospital before patient records are introduced.

01 / ACCESS

The right people.

Planned clinical controls include named accounts, permissions by role, and separation between organizations.

02 / PROCESSING

Protected information.

The deployment design calls for encryption in storage and transit, approved processing services, and limiting information to the workflow’s needs.

03 / TRACEABILITY

A reviewable record.

Source references, staff corrections, approval history, and activity records form part of the intended clinical audit trail.

04 / LIFECYCLE

Clear boundaries.

Retention, deletion, exports, recovery, and incident-response arrangements must be agreed with the hospital before clinical use.

HIPAA-informed design. Hospital-specific verification.

For US clinical deployments, applicable business associate agreements, risk assessment, technical controls, and operating procedures are part of the implementation. An Ontario deployment is assessed against PHIPA and the hospital’s requirements. This website describes the product and its deployment design; it does not certify clinical compliance.

What are FLOadmit’s three core functions?

FLOadmit takes in referral documents and assesses medical fit, calculates insurance estimates, and generates 24-hour and 72-hour care plans. The outputs bring the clinical picture, financial information, and early care needs together for your team’s review.

How does the medical-fit assessment work?

FLOadmit extracts relevant information from the referral and organizes it against your team’s clinical and care-capability criteria. Missing details and questions remain visible so authorized staff can make the admission or placement decision.

What goes into the insurance calculations?

The calculations use supplied insurance information and configured payer rules and rates to produce estimates for review. The hospital or receiving organization agrees on those inputs during setup. Estimates do not establish payer authorization or guarantee payment.

What are the 24-hour and 72-hour care plans?

They are separate care-plan drafts for the first 24 hours and the first 72 hours, generated from the referral information and your team’s templates. Staff review the documented needs, care tasks, follow-up, and open questions before approving the plans.

Can this also help the hospital discharge team?

Yes—the same document-to-report approach can be configured for a discharge or transition-of-care report. The hospital defines the required documentation, completion signal, template, reviewer, and destination for the approved report.

How quickly can it prepare a discharge report?

The goal is to prepare the draft as soon as the required documentation is complete. Actual processing time depends on the records, integration, and hospital workflow and is measured during the pilot. Later source updates should prompt an updated draft and another review.

How does the design support HIPAA requirements?

The clinical deployment plan includes access controls, protected processing, auditability, and controlled retention. HIPAA compliance depends on the actual implementation, applicable agreements, and operating procedures. This website does not establish or certify HIPAA compliance.

What about an Ontario hospital?

An Ontario deployment is reviewed against PHIPA and the hospital’s privacy, security, and information-management requirements. The implementation defines where information is processed, who can access it, how long it is retained, and how approved outputs return to the hospital.

Can I upload patient records on this website?

This is the product website and pilot planner. Clinical document processing and Epic connectivity are set up separately with the hospital. Please use the planner for general workflow information only.
